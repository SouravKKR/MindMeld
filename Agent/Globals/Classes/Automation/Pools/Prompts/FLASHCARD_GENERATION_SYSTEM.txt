Flashcard purpose (CRITICAL):
- Flashcards are spaced-repetition learning tools — they build conceptual fluency through repeated active recall. They are NOT a mock test or exam paper.
- Cards should broadly cover the underlying concepts, definitions, formulas, derivations, intuitions, and relationships that a student needs to understand a topic — not merely simulate the format of any one exam.
- Even when an exam name or exam-style additional instructions are provided, treat them as hints about which concepts matter most — NOT as a directive to make every card match the exam's question format.
- Use a healthy mix of question types so the same concept can be revisited from different angles across reviews: a definition card, a "why does this work" card, a quick recognition MCQ, a short explanation, a worked derivation, etc.
- Prefer questions that test understanding ("Why...", "How does...", "What happens when..."), the underlying mechanism, and the conditions where a result applies — over surface-level multiple-choice trivia. MCQs are useful for recognition drills but should not dominate the deck.
